\label{sec:QGP}

The prior discussion of QCD involved hadrons in a vaccum of zero temperature, however, studying strong dynamics at non-zero (high) temperatures or baryon densities offers a novel enviroment for testing QCD.
This QCD enviroment is very rare in the nature, only being found in two places~\cite{QGP:instantons}.
The first is in the interioir of neutron stars, where the baryon density greatly exceeds normal nucleon densities.
The second is around $10^{-5}$ s after the big bang when the termperature of the entire universe was very large, comprable to that of nucleon rest mass.
Additionally, this high temperature regieme can be syntesized in a laboratory with collisions of relativistic heavy ions.

Models have long identified that nuclear matter is influenced by the thermodynamic enviroment.
The Bootsteap model by Hagedorn~\cite{QGP:Hagedorn}, pre-acceptance of the Parton model, posits that the number of available hadronic states incresed exponetially with the temopertaure of a system.
The result of this is that at a given temperature, $T_H$, that partition function for the hadronic states diverge and any additional energy input into the system will create additional hadrons but will not increase the temperature.
The Bootstrap model estimated this temperature to be roughly $T_H = 160$ \MeV.

With the discuvery of the Asympttic freedom it was discovered that the hadronic couplings will become weak at high temperatures and lead to a state of matter where the degrees of freedom go from hadrons to quarks and gluons.
This transition is related to the spontaeous breaking of the chiral symmetry of QCD~\cite{QGP:Review}.
This state of nuclear matter, originally described in Reference~\cite{QGP:AsymFreeQuarks} as ``quark soup'', has become to be known as Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP).
This led to the reinturpetation of $T_H$ instead as the a phase transistion point, i.e. the critical temperature above which quarks are liberated from hadrons~\cite{QGP:TH}.

The phase diagram for QCD matter is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_phase_diagram}.
At low baryon density the crossover between the hadronic phase and the QGP is a smooth trasnistion.
The nature of this trasistion is a result of the non-zero mass for the light quarks ($d,\,u,\,s$) as for zero light quark masses, calculations show this would be a first-order phase transision.
As the phase diagram moves towards higher $\mu$ the transition Lattice calculations show this becomes a first order phase transition~\cite{QGP:Lattice2}.
At very high baryon density exotic states such as a color superconductor is potstuated to exist, but this portion of the phase diagram is less well studied~\cite{QGP:superconductor}.
Heavy ion collisions at higher and higher energies probe the region of the QGP diagram at low-$\mu$ and high-$T$, because the ratio of baryon number to entropy scales inversely with collision energy.
