\subsection{Signatures of the quark-gluon plasma in small systems}
\label{sec:qgp_small}

The umbrella term ``small systems'' covers many different collision scenarios, each with its own advantages and challenges.
Generally, \pxp, \pxA, \AxAlight, and peripheral \AxA\ collisions can all be considered small systems.
Here, \AxAlight\ refers to light-ion collisions with mass number $A\lesssim 30$.
Light-ion data have only recently become available at RHIC (2021,2026) and the LHC (2025).

Evidence for QGP formation includes partonic energy loss and collective flow.
As the system size decreases from that of central \AxA\ collisions, these experimental signatures are expected to weaken as the fireball, if formed, becomes progressively smaller.
Generally speaking, experimental evidence shows that there is collective flow in small systems but not jet quenching.
Embedding these two experimental trends into a QCD-based model that can effectively describe different observables in data is a main subject of research in high-energy nuclear physics.

\subsubsection{Collectivity in small systems}

Flow-like signatures have been observed in \pxp~\cite{HION-2015-09,HION-2016-01,CMS-HIN-16-010}, \pxPb~\cite{HION-2012-13,CMS-HIN-13-002}, \pxAu, \dxAu~\cite{STAR:OOFlow}, and \HexAu~\cite{PHENIX:SmallFlow}, \OxO, \NexNe~\cite{HION-2025-02,CMS-HIN-25-009,ALICE:OOFlow,STAR:OOFlow}, and peripheral \PbxPb~\cite{HION-2016-06,CMS-HIN-13-002,ALICE:PBPBFlow}.
Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_flow_systems} shows that experimental data from small to large system size can be described by the superSONIC model~\cite{SmallSystemsFlow}.
This suggests that the observed non-zero flow coefficients have a hydrodynamic origin in collisions from \pxp\ to \PbxPb.

Collectivity has also been studied in systems even ``smaller'' than those listed above, such as $\gamma A$ collisions at the LHC by ATLAS where non-zero flow coefficients were also found~\cite{HION-2018-27}.
This can be explained by the propensity for a photon to fluctuate into a resolved vector meson and act hadronically with the nucleus.
The collectivity signal in $e^{+}e^{-}$ collisions measured by the ALEPH detector was not significant and could be explained by \textsc{Pythia}6~\cite{Belle:collective}.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\pxp}{pp} collisions as a no-quenching baseline}

Jet energy loss is not observed in \pxp\ collisions, as demonstrated by the success of pQCD calculations in describing \pxp\ data.
For example, measurements of the balance between an electroweak boson ($\gamma$ or $Z$) and a recoiling jet show that, on average, the boson and jet momenta agree with NLO predictions~\cite{STDM-2011-27}.
Since the electroweak boson does not interact strongly with the medium, any partonic energy loss would affect only the jet.
Therefore, jet quenching in \pxp\ collisions would produce a net momentum imbalance that would disagree with pQCD predictions.
Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_observables}(a) shows that the photon--jet momentum balance in \pxp\ collisions is consistent with pQCD predictions, while the corresponding \PbxPb\ data exhibit an imbalance~\cite{HION-2018-06}.

\subsubsection{Ambiguity of quenching signals in peripheral \texorpdfstring{\AxA}{AA} collisions}

Jet-quenching observables in peripheral \AxA\ collisions show suppression relative to \pxp\ collisions; however, interpreting this suppression is challenging.
Glauber modeling of peripheral collisions is associated with larger uncertainties because, at large impact parameters, the $N_{\mathrm{elem}}$ dependence is dominated by multiplicity fluctuations rather than geometry.
Additionally, events containing a hard process are correlated with higher soft underlying-event activity~\cite{CentBias:periphBias}.
This effect is small at large \NPART, where it is largely washed out, but its relative impact increases as \NPART\ decreases.
In the right panel of Figure~\ref{fig:atlas_hadron_jet_raa}, the jet \Raa\ is approximately $0.8$ at a \PT\ of 50 \GeV, but substantial uncertainties in \TAA\ (about 10\%) complicate its interpretation.
For very peripheral collisions (greater than 80\% centrality), the right panel of Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_observables} shows that the apparent suppression of charged-hadron \Raa\ in \PbxPb\ collisions can be described by a \pythia\ toy model with a HIJING Glauber initial state and no jet quenching~\cite{ALICE:periphBias,CentBias:periphBias}.
Beyond \Raa, correlation measurements (such as dijets and $\gamma$+jet pairs) have been shown to be more robust against centrality biases~\cite{CentBias:OO}.
Figure~\ref{fig:xj_absolute_yields} shows that the yield of balanced dijets is slightly suppressed relative to \pxp\ in the 60--80\% centrality interval, although the significance of this deviation has not been evaluated~\cite{HION-2018-28}.
The most peripheral \Jaa\ measurement reported is for the 40--60\% centrality interval, where the ratio is clearly different from unity~\cite{HION-2024-03}.
The effects of nPDFs and centrality biases on these measurements have not been rigorously evaluated, so definitive conclusions about the presence of jet quenching remain elusive.

\subsubsection{Complications to jet quenching measurements in \texorpdfstring{\pxA}{pA} collisions}
\label{sec:jetquenchingpA}
Measurements of inclusive-hadron and jet \Raa\ in \pxPb\ collisions at the LHC and \dxAu\ collisions at RHIC show no clear jet-quenching signal, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_pPb_Rp}.
The charged-hadron \Raa\ is approximately unity for hadrons with \PT\ > 5 \GeV and is consistent with the two pQCD predictions shown.
The suppression at very low \PT\ can be explained by nPDF effects.
The jet \Raa\ is at or above unity in measurements by ATLAS~\cite{HION-2013-08}, ALICE~\cite{ALICE:jetpPb}, CMS~\cite{CMS-HIN-14-001}, and PHENIX~\cite{PHENIX:jetpPb}.

Centrality-differential jet production has also been studied by PHENIX in \dxAu\ collisions and by ATLAS in \pxPb\ collisions~\cite{PHENIX:jetpPb,HION-2013-08}.
The centrality-differential \Raa\ in \dxAu\ collisions is presented in the left panel of Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_centrality_jets}.
Focusing initially on the most central 0--20\% \dxAu\ class, the suppressed \Raa\ is compatible with a small jet-quenching signal and is compared with a model that includes medium-induced radiation~\cite{pAEnergyLossModel}.
However, this model failed to describe the observed enhancement of the \Raa\ in peripheral collisions well above what could be expected from nPDF effects.
These \Raa\ findings were qualitatively similar to the ATLAS measurement~\cite{HION-2013-08}.
ATLAS defined the central-to-peripheral ratio of jet yields, \RCP, as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:RCP}
    R_{\mathrm{CP}} =
    \frac{\displaystyle
        \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{evt}}\langle T_{p\mathrm{Pb}}\rangle}
        \frac{ \mathrm{d}^2N}
    {\mathrm{d}p_{\mathrm{T}}\,\mathrm{d}y} \bigg |_{\mathrm{central}}}
    {\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{evt}}\langle T_{p+\mathrm{Pb}}\rangle}
        \frac{ \mathrm{d}^2N}
    {\mathrm{d}p_{\mathrm{T}}\,\mathrm{d}y} \bigg |_{\mathrm{peripheral}}}
\end{equation}

This represents the ratio of central to peripheral jet yields, corrected for the difference in \NCOLL\ between the two classes.
The right panel of Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_centrality_jets} shows that the \RCP\ decreases as a function of the \textit{total jet energy}, defined as \PT$\times \cosh\left(\langle y^* \rangle\right)$, where \YSTAR\ is the average rapidity of all jets in the event.

This suppression of \RCP\ as a function of jet energy was interpreted as a color-transparency effect (see Section~\ref{sec:CT}) rather than as evidence of jet energy loss.
Events with higher jet energy are more likely to have been initiated by a high-$x$ parton in the proton and therefore to involve a spatially compact transverse configuration, leading to fewer nucleon--nucleon collisions.
This centrality bias, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:pAcentBias}, directly causes events to migrate to more peripheral centrality intervals, decreasing the \RCP.
ATLAS studied this effect in more detail by measuring the triple-differential dijet yield in 8.16 \TeV\ \pxPb\ collisions~\cite{HION-2023-05}.
The dijet system can be related to the Bjorken-$x$ of the proton through the average transverse momentum (\PTA), the average rapidity (\YBOOST), and the half-angle separation in rapidity (\YSTAR).
Figure~\ref{fig:atlas_xp_rcp} shows that the \RCP\ scales directly with the proton's $x$, further supporting the color-transparency hypothesis.

    \caption{ATLAS jet central-to-peripheral ratio, \RCP, as a function of proton momentum fraction $x_p$ in 8.16~\TeV\ \pxPb\ collisions~\cite{HION-2023-05}.}
    \label{fig:atlas_xp_rcp}
\end{figure}

The observed suppression of the central \Raa\ in \dxAu\ and \pxPb\ can be explained by an event-activity bias arising from color-transparency effects.
Since any jet-quenching signal in \pxA\ is expected to be small, and inclusive \pxA\ measurements show no such evidence, it is of great interest to perform less-biased, centrality-differentiated measurements.
One proposed way to reduce the CT bias is to use centrality estimators that are less sensitive to CT than event activity~\cite{Dennis:EABias}\footnote{Event activity at forward rapidities has been used as the centrality estimator in the measurements mentioned above.}.
One such CE selects centrality classes in \pxA\ events based on the number of spectator neutrons measured at $|\eta|>8$ by a Zero-Degree Calorimeter (ZDC) located in the nucleus-going direction~\cite{HION-2021-17,ALICE:ZdcCent}.
This dissertation includes a measurement that directly probes the impact of CT effects on two CEs, event activity and forward neutrons, as a function of the Bjorken-$x$ of the parton extracted from the proton in the hard scattering.
This inverts the previous approach~\cite{HION-2013-08,HION-2023-05} by directly measuring the quantities used to estimate centrality as a function of the jet kinematics, providing the first quantitative investigation of global observables in the presence of CF effects.
The details of this analysis are presented in~\ref{ch:pPb} of this dissertation.

\subsubsection{Jet quenching in \texorpdfstring{\AxAlight}{AAlight} collisions}
Light-ion collisions provide a fertile new system for searching for the onset of partonic energy loss.
As seen in Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_systemsize_theory}, minimum-bias \OxO\ collisions have a similar \NPART\ to peripheral \PbxPb\ collisions, where observing jet quenching is precluded by the aforementioned issues.
Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_systemsize_theory} also shows hadron and jet \Raa\ measurements in several systems as a function of \NPART.
Following the trends of the experimental points and taking experimental uncertainties into account, this indicates that the \Raa\ for minimum-bias \OxO\ collisions should be around 0.8.
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=0.6\linewidth,

    \caption{\NCOLL\ and \NPART\ for different collision species at the LHC; \Raa\ as a function of \NPART\ for LHC jet and hadron data.  Taken from Reference~\cite{SmallSystems:Theory1}.}
    \label{fig:qgp_small_systemsize_theory}
\end{figure}

However, this argument based simply on \NPART\ is weak, as it ignores the geometry of the collision system.
The $\hat{q}$ parameter is a function of path length in the medium~\cite{QGP:Ldep}, not \NCOLL.
For hard probes measured near midrapidity, the transverse extent of the overlap region controls the path length.
For central collisions, the radius of the QGP formed in an A+B nuclear collision is approximately limited by the radius of the smaller nucleus.
Table~\ref{tab:small_system_radii} summarizes the radii of all collision species that have collided at the LHC.
Light-ion collisions, such as \OxO\ and \NexNe, provide a bridge between systems with small and large transverse sizes.

As an aside, these transverse size arguments can shed light on why jet quenching is so elusive in \pxPb.
A medium created in \pxPb\ collisions cannot be much larger than 1 fm since it is limited by the proton size.
A jet could very easily escape from the overlap region before the hydrodynamic stage, given that a QGP formation time is $\approx$1 fm/c.

ALICE and CMS reported \Roo\ results in \OxO\ collisions shortly after the data became available~\cite{CMS-HIN-25-008,ALICE:ROO}.
The CMS results (left panel) show the evolution of the charged-hadron \Raa\ from \pxPb\ to \PbxPb\, with the \Roo\ falling squarely in between the two.
The \Roo\ is significantly below unity over a broad range of \PT. Compared with models incorporating only nPDF effects and models incorporating both nPDF effects and jet quenching, the data favor the model that includes jet quenching.
The ALICE measurement (right panel) shows that the $\pi^0$ \Roo\ is suppressed relative to nPDF-only baseline scenarios; however, this effect is less pronounced at high \PT\ when experimental uncertainties are taken into account.
ALICE also reports an \Raa\ in \pxO\ collisions, based on the expectation that if no jet quenching is observed in \pxPb\ then there should also be none in an even smaller system \pxO.
The \Rpo\ is consistent with unity across a broad range of \PT.
All of these \Raa\ measurements are minimum-bias measurements (using the left expression in Equation~\ref{eq:RAA}), meaning they have no Glauber model dependence.
This lack of centrality differentiation is motivated by expected centrality biases for \Raa\ measurements in light-ion collisions~\cite{CentBias:OO}.

The CMS results report suppression relative to unity with a significance greater than $5\sigma$, while the ALICE results report a significance of $9.2\sigma$.
To further constrain nPDF effects, ALICE measures the double ratio \RooDrpoSq\ to factor out cold nuclear matter effects coming from the oxygen nuclei.
The factor of two accounts for the two nuclei participating in the \OxO\ collision\footnote{Note that the \OxO\ and \pxO\ collisions were taken at different values of \SNN, so this ratio does not account for the \SNN\ dependence of nPDFs}.
Taking this ratio greatly reduces the impact of nPDFs on the interpretation, as shown in the left panel of Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_oxygen_double_ratio_icp}.
The double ratio is suppressed beyond the nPDF-only predictions and is consistent with calculations from two separate jet-quenching models.
Taking nPDF uncertainties into account, the double ratio is suppressed with a significance of $4.9\sigma$.

At RHIC, STAR measured the semi-inclusive yield of hadrons and jets in \OxO\ collisions at 200 \GeV~\cite{Star:ICP}.
Figure~\ref{fig:qgp_small_oxygen_double_ratio_icp} (right panel) shows the central-to-peripheral ratio of semi-inclusive jets ($I_{\mathrm{CP}}$).
The jets used in the STAR analysis are charged-particle jets, which lack sensitivity to neutral hadrons.
The numerator selection in the ratio is varied, while the peripheral selection is always 40--60\%.
For the most central selection (0--10\%), the suppression of $I_{\mathrm{CP}}$ relative to unity has a significance of $4.3\sigma$, increasing to $5\sigma$ when the most extreme nPDF effects are considered.
Using a correlation observable (hadron-triggered jets) instead of a spectrum (\Raa) is expected to mitigate centrality biases in light-ion collisions~\cite{CentBias:OO}.
However, using peripheral data as a reference can be susceptible to projectile-configuration biases affecting the nucleon from which the jet is extracted; these biases can appear as a reduction in the central-to-peripheral ratio~\cite{HION-2023-05}.

Concurrently with the light-ion jet-quenching searches presented above, the author of this dissertation was the main analyzer for an ATLAS measurement of dijet balance in \OxO\ and \NexNe\ collisions~\cite{HION-2025-16}.
Since this analysis is of the correlations of dijets, the quenching observable can be studied differentially in centrality with minimal centrality-bias effects~\cite{CentBias:OO}.
By measuring the dijet balance across centralities, the analysis correlates the onset of jet quenching with increasing nuclear overlap.
The measurement includes a reference \pxp\ sample taken at the same energy and with similar detector conditions; therefore, it avoids potential biases from projectile configurations while reducing experimental uncertainties.
Furthermore, this is the first analysis of \OxO\ data utilizing fully reconstructed calorimeter jets.
ATLAS has a great deal of institutional knowledge from past measurements of dijet balance~\cite{HION-2010-02,HION-2012-11,HION-2019-02,HION-2018-28,HION-2024-03}, and has focused that expertise on a fast analysis of \OxO\ collisions, culminating in a preliminary result two months post data-taking.
That preliminary result, presented by the author at the Initial Stages 2025 conference in Taipei, was quickly followed by a final publication in June 2026.
The details of this analysis are presented in~\ref{ch:xj} of this dissertation.
